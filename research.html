Research Interests

Ancient epic and mythology; the social history of the Roman world, with attention to slavery and emotional life; the material culture of the ancient Mediterranean, particularly ceramic evidence recovered from mortuary contexts; and the reception of antiquity in modern film and popular culture, especially tabletop and digital games. His senior thesis, currently at the proposal stage, examines the use of dice in ancient Rome — their manufacture, their role in games of chance, and their place in Roman religious and social life. His interests sit at the intersection of close textual and object-based work and the broader cultural questions that evidence raises — how myth and play shape a society's understanding of itself, and how antiquity continues to be reimagined through games today.

Senior Thesis

The Use of Dice in Ancient Rome

Trinity College, Classical Studies Department Proposal stage

An examination of dice and games of chance in Roman life, considering their material construction, their social and religious significance, and what they reveal about Roman attitudes toward fortune and risk.

Research Experience

Research Assistant to Prof. Martha Risser

Trinity College · Isthmia, Greece May 2026 – Present

On-site at Isthmia's West Cemetery, an Ancient Corinthian graveyard, analyzing pottery from the 7th–4th centuries BCE recovered in and around the graves. Work includes determining style, date, and dimensions of sherds to help reconstruct evidence about the people buried there; organizing and cataloguing research boxes; and cross-referencing finds against spreadsheets for publication in Prof. Risser's forthcoming book.

Research Assistant to Prof. Sarah Levin-Richardson

University of Washington May – Aug 2025

Assisted on Roman Slavery and Emotional Labor, a forthcoming study of enslaved people's affective and social experience in ancient Rome. Copyedited more than 65 pages of manuscript, translated Greek and Latin source passages, and verified over 100 citations for accuracy and completeness ahead of publication.

Research Assistant to Prof. Vincent Tomasso

Trinity College, Classical Studies Department May – Sep 2024

Contributed research toward a forthcoming book on classical epic, prepared class materials and text summaries, and advised incoming students on Latin placement. Also worked with department faculty to reinstate Trinity's mythology minor, helping shape the curriculum available to future students of myth.
