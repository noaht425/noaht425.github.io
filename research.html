Research Interests

His central interest is the reception of antiquity in modern games, whether board games, tabletop roleplaying games, or video games. He wants to know what these adaptations keep of the ancient world and what they change, and what players come to believe about myth and fate from the version of it built into a game's rules. His other interests include ancient epic and mythology, the social history of the Roman world with attention to slavery and emotional life, and the material culture of the ancient Mediterranean, particularly ceramic evidence recovered from mortuary contexts. His senior thesis, on the use of dice in ancient Rome and what survives of Roman attitudes toward chance in modern play, is where these interests meet.

Senior Thesis

The Use of Dice in Ancient Rome

Trinity College · Advised by Prof. Vincent Tomasso In progress, 2026–27

The thesis argues that ancient players and oracle-seekers shared an assumption: a throw of the dice was more than chance. The first half works entirely from ancient evidence, in three chapters. One takes up the dice themselves, how they were made and why so many of them are lopsided. The second takes up cheating and the other attempts to control a throw. The third turns to divination, and to the inscribed oracle charts of Asia Minor that assign each throw of the knucklebones to a god. The second half follows the same assumption into modern tabletop play, where players still treat dice as something that can be persuaded or punished. Sources include Suetonius, Juvenal, Ammianus, and Pausanias alongside recent work by Purcell, Schädler, Graf, and Eerkens and de Voogt.

Projects

Omphalos

Tabletop roleplaying system set in the ancient Greek world 2025 – Present

A tabletop roleplaying game he is writing and designing, set in the Greek world of the late fifth century BC. Characters advance by earning kleos instead of experience points, gaining it for deeds such as helping strangers or dealing with monsters, so progress in the game is measured the way the Homeric poems measure it. The rules stay inside the period as far as the evidence allows: equipment and prices are pinned to Athenian coinage, and the witchcraft recipes were written after consulting texts such as the Greek Magical Papyri. The game also takes up how ancient views of gender and monstrosity differ from ours, including the Greek habit of making monsters out of women. Art is being commissioned from several artists ahead of a planned Kickstarter.
