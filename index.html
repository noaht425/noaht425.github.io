About

Noah Turner is an undergraduate at Trinity College reading Classical Studies, with a minor in English. His coursework spans Latin and Attic Greek, Greek and Roman art and architecture, mythology, archaeological chemistry, and the afterlives of antiquity on film. Since May 2026 he has worked on-site at Isthmia, Greece, as a research assistant to Professor Martha Risser, analyzing and cataloguing seventh- to fourth-century BCE pottery excavated from the Ancient Corinthian graveyard at Isthmia's West Cemetery for her forthcoming book.

His research experience also includes assisting Professor Vincent Tomasso on a forthcoming book concerning classical epic, and working with Professor Sarah Levin-Richardson at the University of Washington on Roman Slavery and Emotional Labor, where he translated Greek and Latin sources and verified more than one hundred citations for a work now heading to publication. At Trinity, he has served as a teaching assistant and as the Classical Studies department's ambassador. He is currently developing his senior thesis on the use of dice in ancient Rome, and is preparing to apply to graduate programs in Classics, with particular interests in ancient epic, mythology, material culture, and the reception of antiquity in modern games.
